📖 BFSI Domain Dictionary — Must Know All 12
NPA
Non-Performing Asset. Loan where EMI unpaid >90 days.
NPA Rate = NPA Loans / Total Loans × 100
Churn Rate
% customers who stopped using a product in a period.
Churned / Start-of-period customers × 100
LTV
Lifetime Value. Total revenue a customer generates over their lifecycle.
Avg Revenue × Avg Tenure (months)
CAC
Customer Acquisition Cost. Cost to onboard 1 new customer.
Total Marketing Spend / New Customers
AUM
Assets Under Management. Total value of assets a bank manages.
Sum of all portfolio values
EMI
Equated Monthly Instalment. Fixed monthly loan repayment amount.
P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1)
CIBIL Score
India's credit score (300–900). >750 = low risk.
Based on repayment history, utilisation
DPD
Days Past Due. How many days a payment is overdue.
DPD = Today - EMI_Due_Date

ROI / ROA
Return on Investment / Assets. Profitability ratios.
Net Profit / Total Assets × 100

Age of loan/customer. Older loans = lower risk (proven payer).

🎓 Theory Q&A — 5 BFSI Interview Questions
Q1. What is the difference between a delinquent customer and an NPA customer? Why does this distinction matter to a bank?
Hired answer: A delinquent customer has missed payments (DPD 1–90 days) but is not yet classified as NPA. An NPA customer has unpaid EMI for more than 90 days — at this point the bank must provision capital against that loan. The distinction matters because: (1) delinquent = recoverable, needs a collections call; (2) NPA = provisioned loss, impacts the bank's P&L and capital adequacy ratio. As an analyst, I segment DPD buckets: 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ to give collections teams their priority list.
Q2. How would you define "churned" in a banking context? Is it the same for savings accounts vs credit cards?
Hired answer: Churn definition varies by product. For a savings account: no transaction in last 6–12 months (dormant account). For a credit card: no swipe in last 3 months OR balance transferred out. For a loan: early prepayment can mean churn (lost future interest). Always agree the churn definition with the business before writing a single query — the wrong definition produces the wrong intervention.
Q3. What is cohort analysis and why is it more useful than overall churn rate?
Hired answer: Cohort analysis groups customers by when they joined (e.g. Jan 2026 cohort) and tracks what % are still active each month. Overall churn rate hides the story — a 10% churn rate could mean: (a) all cohorts churn at 10% uniformly, OR (b) new cohorts churn at 30% but old cohorts are loyal. Cohort analysis separates these. In banking, it shows whether product changes improved retention for newer customers vs older ones — actionable for product and marketing teams.
Q4. A risk manager says "our NPA rate jumped from 3% to 5% this quarter." What are the first 3 questions you ask as a data analyst?
Hired answer: (1) Which product? — Is it concentrated in Loans, Credit Cards, or spread across all? A concentrated spike is a product-design issue. (2) Which vintage? — Are the new NPAs from recent disbursements (bad underwriting) or old loans (economic stress)? (3) Which geography/segment? — Is it region-specific (local economic event) or pan-India? Each answer changes the intervention: tighten credit scoring, increase collections in a region, or pause disbursements in a product.
Q5. What is LTV:CAC ratio and what does a ratio of 1.5 mean for a bank's retail lending product?
Hired answer: LTV (Lifetime Value) is the total revenue a customer generates over their relationship. CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) is what it cost to onboard them. A ratio of 1.5 means the bank earns ₹1.50 for every ₹1 spent acquiring the customer — marginally profitable but dangerous. Best-in-class is 3:1 or higher. At 1.5, any increase in NPA, churn, or servicing cost tips the product into loss. I would flag this to the product team and model the breakeven CAC needed to hit 3:1.
Task 1 — Churn Analysis (40 min)

🎯 Business Ask: Identify which reps/regions have "churned" customers (no sale in last 90 days) and calculate churn rate by region.

For this exercise: a rep is "at-risk" if their last sale was more than 90 days before the latest sale date in the dataset.

Query 1A — Last Activity per Rep + Churn Flag

WITH rep_last_sale AS (
    SELECT
        rep_name,
        MAX(sale_date)                            AS last_sale_date,
        DATEDIFF(
            (SELECT MAX(sale_date) FROM sales),
            MAX(sale_date)
        )                                         AS days_since_last_sale
    FROM sales
    GROUP BY rep_name
),
all_reps AS (
    SELECT
        r.rep_name,
        r.team,
        COALESCE(rl.last_sale_date, '1900-01-01')  AS last_sale_date,
        COALESCE(rl.days_since_last_sale, 9999)     AS days_inactive,
        CASE
            WHEN rl.rep_name IS NULL           THEN 'Churned'
            WHEN rl.days_since_last_sale > 90  THEN 'At Risk'
            WHEN rl.days_since_last_sale > 30  THEN 'Cooling'
            ELSE                                     'Active'
        END                                       AS churn_status
    FROM reps r
    LEFT JOIN rep_last_sale rl
        ON r.rep_name = rl.rep_name
)
SELECT * FROM all_reps
ORDER BY days_inactive DESC;

-- Eve has no sales → LEFT JOIN keeps her → COALESCE fills NULL → 'Churned'
-- Business: Collections/HR gets a prioritised outreach list

Query 1B — Churn Rate by Team

SELECT
    team,
    COUNT(*)                                           AS total_reps,
    SUM(CASE WHEN churn_status IN ('Churned','At Risk')
             THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)                        AS at_risk_count,
    ROUND(
        SUM(CASE WHEN churn_status IN ('Churned','At Risk')
                 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*),
    1)                                                 AS churn_rate_pct
FROM (
    -- paste the all_reps CTE result here or use a subquery
    SELECT r.rep_name, r.team,
           CASE WHEN MAX(s.sale_date) IS NULL
                    OR DATEDIFF((SELECT MAX(sale_date) FROM sales),
                                 MAX(s.sale_date)) > 90
                THEN 'Churned'
                ELSE 'Active' END AS churn_status
    FROM reps r LEFT JOIN sales s ON r.rep_name = s.rep_name
    GROUP BY r.rep_name, r.team
) t
GROUP BY team
ORDER BY churn_rate_pct DESC;

-- Expected: Retail team has higher churn (Eve is Retail, no sales)
-- Business: Which team needs management intervention NOW?
Task 2 — NPA Segmentation (40 min)

🎯 Business Ask: Segment the loan portfolio into DPD buckets. Calculate NPA rate and average revenue at risk per bucket.

We simulate DPD using days since sale_date (as a proxy for loan disbursement).

Query 2A — DPD Bucket Segmentation

SELECT
    sale_id,
    rep_name,
    region,
    product,
    revenue,
    sale_date,
    DATEDIFF(CURDATE(), sale_date)           AS dpd,
    CASE
        WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(), sale_date) > 270 THEN 'NPA (90+ DPD)'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(), sale_date) > 180 THEN 'Sub-Standard (61–90)'
        WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(), sale_date) > 90  THEN 'Watchlist (31–60)'
        ELSE                                          'Standard (0–30)'
    END                                           AS dpd_bucket,
    CASE
        WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(), sale_date) > 270 THEN 'Y'
        ELSE 'N'
    END                                           AS is_npa
FROM sales
WHERE product = 'Loan'
ORDER BY dpd DESC;

Query 2B — NPA Rate Summary by Product

SELECT
    product,
    COUNT(*)                                              AS total_accounts,
    SUM(CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(),sale_date)>270
             THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)                          AS npa_count,
    SUM(CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(),sale_date)>270
             THEN revenue ELSE 0 END)                     AS npa_revenue_at_risk,
    ROUND(
        SUM(CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(CURDATE(),sale_date)>270
                 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*),
    1)                                                    AS npa_rate_pct
FROM sales
GROUP BY product
ORDER BY npa_rate_pct DESC;

-- Business: Risk team uses this to decide provisioning amounts
-- If Loan NPA rate > 5%, regulatory flag in India (RBI guidelines)
Task 3 — Cohort Retention in Python (40 min)

🎯 Business Ask: Build a monthly cohort retention table. Which acquisition month retained customers best?

Full Cohort Analysis — Jupyter Notebook

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# ── Load data ────────────────────────────────
sales = pd.read_csv('sales.csv')
sales['sale_date'] = pd.to_datetime(sales['sale_date'])

# ── Step 1: Cohort month = first sale month per rep ──
cohort_df = sales.groupby('rep_name')['sale_date']\
                 .min().reset_index()
cohort_df.columns = ['rep_name', 'cohort_month']
cohort_df['cohort_month'] = cohort_df['cohort_month']\
                               .dt.to_period('M')

# ── Step 2: Merge cohort month back into sales ──
sales = sales.merge(cohort_df, on='rep_name')
sales['sale_month'] = sales['sale_date'].dt.to_period('M')

# ── Step 3: Month index (0 = acquisition month) ──
sales['month_number'] = (
    sales['sale_month'].astype(int) -
    sales['cohort_month'].astype(int)
)

# ── Step 4: Cohort table ──────────────────────
cohort_table = sales.groupby(
    ['cohort_month', 'month_number']
)['rep_name'].nunique().reset_index()
cohort_pivot = cohort_table.pivot_table(
    index='cohort_month',
    columns='month_number',
    values='rep_name'
)

# ── Step 5: Retention % (divide by cohort size) ──
cohort_size   = cohort_pivot.iloc[:, 0]
retention_pct = cohort_pivot.divide(cohort_size, axis=0) * 100

print("── Cohort Retention % ──")
print(retention_pct.round(1))

# ── Step 6: Business Insight ──────────────────
best_cohort = retention_pct[1].idxmax()  # Month-1 retention
print(f"\nBest retained cohort: {best_cohort}")
print(f"Avg Month-1 retention: {retention_pct[1].mean():.1f}%")

Task 4 — Risk Dashboard in Power BI (50 min)

🎯 Business Ask: Build a risk monitoring dashboard for the Risk Manager. Must show NPA exposure, churn rate, and rep status at a glance.

DAX Measures for Risk Dashboard

-- NPA Rate (simulated via DPD proxy)
NPA Rate % =
DIVIDE(
    COUNTROWS(FILTER(sales,
        DATEDIFF(sales[sale_date], TODAY(), DAY) > 270)),
    COUNTROWS(sales),
    0
)

-- Revenue at Risk
Revenue at Risk =
CALCULATE(
    SUM(sales[revenue]),
    FILTER(sales,
        DATEDIFF(sales[sale_date], TODAY(), DAY) > 90)
)

-- Active Rep Count (sold in last 90 days)
Active Reps =
COUNTROWS(
    FILTER(
        SUMMARIZE(sales, sales[rep_name],
            "LastSale", MAX(sales[sale_date])),
        DATEDIFF([LastSale], TODAY(), DAY) <= 90
    )
)

-- Churn Rate %
Churn Rate % =
DIVIDE(
    COUNTROWS(reps) - [Active Reps],
    COUNTROWS(reps),
    0
)
